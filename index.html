Temperature difference calculatorCalculate cooling split or furnace temperature rise without leaving the app. Offline
AC TEMPERATURE DIFFERENTIAL — REFERENCE GUIDE
Follow the four field steps, then use the calculator. The guide is for quick reference and does not create duplicate inspection records.
1Run the AC for at least 15 minutes
Start the air-conditioning system and allow it to operate continuously for a minimum of 15 minutes before measuring the temperature differential.
2Measure return air at the filter housing
Go to the HVAC filter housing. Remove the filter only as necessary to obtain the return-air temperature at the filter housing, then record the reading in the calculator below.
3Restore the filter before continuing
Reinstall the filter correctly and close the filter housing. Return the system to its normal operating configuration. Do not leave the filter removed while measuring supply air.
4Measure supply air and calculate
Measure supply-air temperature at the first suitable supply register. Enter both readings below; the app calculates return minus supply and gives the configured field-screen interpretation.

Use representative locations, one verified thermometer, and the required run time. Cooling difference is a field screen—not a refrigerant or capacity diagnosis. Heating rise must be compared with the equipment nameplate range.

The well test has reached the required test duration.

1Inform client and occupants before testingEstablish the no-water-use rule before starting.Pending
  • Speak with the client and every occupant, contractor, agent, or other person present before beginning.
  • Advise everyone that no water may be used anywhere on the property for the full 100-minute test.
  • This includes toilets, sinks, showers, bathtubs, washing machines, dishwashers, exterior faucets, and every other water-using fixture or appliance.
  • Confirm that everyone understands before starting.
2Identify water discharge locationSelect a safe spigot and discharge route.Pending
  • Locate an operational exterior hose bib/spigot suitable for testing.
  • If a suitable spigot is available at the well equipment, determine whether the discharge hose can be routed safely outside.
  • Confirm the discharge will not cause flooding, erosion, water intrusion, or another unsafe condition.
3Locate and inspect well equipmentDocument the pressure tank, plumbing, and visible condition.Pending
  • Locate the accessible well equipment in the basement, crawlspace, utility room, well house, well pit, or other location.
  • Photograph the pressure tank and its manufacturer label.
  • Record the pressure tank manufacturer, model, and capacity where available.
  • Identify the visible plumbing material and check the tank, piping, fittings, and related equipment for leakage, corrosion, damage, or abnormal conditions.
4Inspect pressure controlsRecord switch range, actual pressures, and gauge response.Pending
  • Locate the pressure switch and pressure gauge.
  • Identify the pressure-switch operating range where possible, such as 20/40, 30/50, or 40/60 PSI.
  • Photograph the pressure switch and record its setting.
  • Record actual pump cut-in and cut-out pressures and confirm the gauge responds as pressure changes.
  • Document abnormal pressure fluctuation or rapid cycling.
5Set up pump amperage monitoringPosition the clamp meter safely on one energized conductor.Pending
  • Set the clamp meter to the appropriate AC amperage setting.
  • Clamp around one individual energized conductor supplying the well pump.
  • Position the meter so running amperage can be observed safely during testing.
  • Avoid contact with exposed energized electrical components.
6Set up yield-test equipmentConnect, route, photograph, and record the initial totalizer.Pending
  • Connect the well-yield equipment to the selected spigot and route the discharge hose safely outside.
  • Confirm all connections are secure.
  • Photograph the totalizing/yield meter before starting and record the initial reading.
  • Confirm again that no water is being used anywhere on the property.
7Begin the 100-minute testOpen the spigot, start the timer, and record initial flow.Pending
  • Fully open the test spigot and immediately start the 100-minute timer.
  • Confirm water is flowing through the flow meter.
  • Photograph the operating flow meter and record the initial flow rate in GPM.
8Record pump operationCapture cut-in, amperage, and operating behavior.Pending
  • Observe pressure falling until the pressure switch activates the pump and record cut-in pressure.
  • Once the pump is operating steadily, record running amperage and photograph the reading.
  • Observe for excessive cycling, pressure fluctuation, vibration, unusual noise, air, bubbles, or other abnormal operation.
9Monitor during the testQuick checks at approximately 15-minute intervals.Pending
  • At each interval check flow rate, pump amperage, system pressure, cycling, and overall operation.
  • Observe discharged water for air, bubbles, sediment, discoloration, or loss of flow.
  • Record significant changes and remain alert for unauthorized water use inside the property.
